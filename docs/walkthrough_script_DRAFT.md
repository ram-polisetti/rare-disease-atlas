# DRAFT: Walkthrough video script (about 60 to 90 seconds)

**Status: DRAFT, awaiting Charan's review. Charan records and narrates this himself. Nothing here is final without his go-ahead.**

Updated 2026-10-04 to match the site as deployed: 11 tabs, charcoal-gray theme, reading-level switch, accessibility panel, print reports.

Record against the live demo at https://ram-polisetti.github.io/rare-disease-atlas/ (or locally with `cd site && python3 -m http.server 8000`). Zoom the browser to about 125% so labels read clearly.

Click path: **Guide search > narrow by symptoms > Your Disease > Explore evidence panel > Endpoints > What If > The 10x Case.**

## Script

**0:00 to 0:08, hook** [Atlas loads on the Guide tab.]
"Most rare diseases affect so few people that no one community can build the trials and registries it needs alone. This atlas finds the diseases that share biology and measurements, so that work can be shared."

**0:08 to 0:22, Guide** [Type a parent-style query, for example "my son is losing words and has coarse facial features". Point at the "You asked / We understood" trail.]
"A parent can type what they see, in their own words. The atlas shows what it understood, so nothing is a black box. Then a few symptom checkboxes narrow the list live. Every match stays visible, and the Guide always ends in one clear next step: talk to a geneticist, with a printable summary to bring."

**0:22 to 0:32, Your Disease** [Open Sanfilippo A (MPS IIIA).]
"Here is Sanfilippo A: the gene, SGSH, its symptoms, active trials, patient groups, and treatment status. Fayuvi, the gene therapy approved on September 17, 2026, is shown with who qualifies and who may fall outside the label."

**0:32 to 0:44, Explore** [Switch to Explore, click an edge or node, show the evidence panel.]
"Everything comes from a knowledge graph. Click any link and the evidence panel shows the source, the exact quote, and a plain confidence label: stated by a curated database, stated in research, or only suggested by analysis."

**0:44 to 0:56, Endpoints** [Open Endpoints, show the sharing matrix.]
"Endpoints shows which diseases could use the same measurements in trials, like heparan sulfate across the MPS diseases. And it says the important part plainly: sharing a measurement is not sharing biology."

**0:56 to 1:06, What If** [Open What If, pick a disease from the 51-disease picker, show Scenario A.]
"What If lets an advocate pick any of the 51 diseases and see what a natural-history study or a newborn-screening push would involve: collaborators, trials, and the gaps still to fill."

**1:06 to 1:18, The 10x Case** [Open The 10x Case and show the two bars.]
"And one fully worked example. In our model, a Sanfilippo A natural-history study built from scratch takes about 5.5 years. Reusing the MPS I and II design and pooling subtypes brings it to about 2.2. These are modeled estimates, not measured results, and every assumption is on the page."

**1:18 to 1:25, close** [Toggle Plain language, then open the Accessibility panel briefly.]
"Plain-language reading, accessibility options, print-ready reports. Every claim traceable to its source. That is the AI Atlas for Rare Diseases."

## Recording notes

- Keep each beat on time or the close gets cut. If the cap is 60 seconds, drop the What If beat.
- Pause narration during clicks so each one registers on screen.
- Say "modeled" every time the 5.5 and 2.2 numbers come up. Do not call them results.
- Do not say any disease has no treatment.
- Check the live site the morning of recording, since the site was still being fixed on 2026-10-04.
